04 / Agent memory & state

Memory changes future behavior

Memory is not merely “more context.”

01

TYPE — transient context, session state, workflow state, durable institutional state, episodic/semantic/procedural memory or external source of truth?

02

WRITE AUTHORITY — who may create, update, summarize, overwrite, invalidate or delete?

03

READ AUTHORITY — which principal, task, tenant, workflow or agent may retrieve it?

04

PROVENANCE — where did it come from, when, and what transformed it?

05

FRESHNESS — when does it expire or require revalidation?

06

CONFLICT — what happens when memory disagrees with current truth?

07

TRUST — evidence, untrusted context, hint or authoritative state?

08

ISOLATION — can it cross user, customer, tenant, case or workflow boundaries?

09

POISONING — can hostile/incorrect content be written and later influence action?

10

RETENTION — what is retained, summarized, archived or deleted, and by whom?

11

AUTHORITY — memory must not silently manufacture effective authority.

12

RECOVERY — what happens after crash, rollback, policy/model change or incident?

Remembered information may influence reasoning, but memory must not silently manufacture authority or become evidence merely because the agent retained it.
05 / Operational accountability

Control the integrated system, not the story it tells about itself

Structural evidence and authority boundaries must survive model misbehavior.

Audit awareness may influence behavior; auditability must not depend on agent cooperation.
Normal compliant operation preserves bounded autonomy. Material deviation can contract authority, but containment must be balanced against continuity.
Model-dependent behavior may change. System-enforced authority invariants must not silently change with the model.
Containment is itself consequential. A false-positive detector that quarantines legitimate work can cause an outage. Deviation policy therefore requires thresholds, confidence, false-positive cost, false-negative cost, degraded operating modes and explicit restoration.
06 / Feasibility budget

Must · Should · Stretch

v0.7 uses scope budgets rather than calendar deadlines. Mastery does not expire because a stage takes longer.

Must

Pass-critical

Required to demonstrate the stage capability. Missing a Must blocks the gate.

Should

Deepening

High-value work used when the learner needs additional depth or evidence. It does not automatically block progression.

Stretch

Non-blocking

Useful specialization or extra practice. Stretch work must never silently become a graduation requirement.

One 10-day book at a time. No calendar debt. No unnecessary restart. Scope is adjusted through books; standards are not diluted.
07 / Measurable evidence gates

Four-level rubric

“PASS requires evidence” is now operationalized.

0

Not demonstrated

Cannot perform the capability or produces materially incorrect/unsafe work.

1

Assisted / fragile

Can progress only with substantial prompting, copied structure or repeated rescue.

2

Independent / sufficient

Performs the capability independently, explains choices, handles ordinary variation and produces required evidence.

3

Robust / adaptive

Handles ambiguity, changed requirements, adversarial challenge and trade-offs without losing control of scope or evidence.

Gate rule

PASS = every critical dimension ≥ 2 + no critical safety/control defect + required reconstruction/variation succeeds.

Quantitative coverage may supplement a rubric when a scenario has a predefined hidden-requirement key, but missing one catastrophic requirement can still fail the gate even if numerical coverage is high.

Discovery
  • problem framing
  • hidden-constraint elicitation
  • stakeholder/decision-right discovery
  • assumption tracking
  • avoidance of premature solutioning
  • summary/confirmation quality
Build
  • independent construction
  • correct mechanism
  • observability
  • failure handling
  • reconstruction/variation
Defense
  • why this design
  • where enforcement lives
  • credible alternative rejected
  • failure prevented
  • limitation/residual risk
From C4
  • property stated
  • assumptions stated
  • check chosen appropriately
  • result interpreted
  • what was not proved stated
Stage-specific scoring: the Build & Execution thread may instantiate these dimensions into scenario-specific indicators, but it may not lower the pass threshold or invent new pass-critical capabilities outside the active stage.
08 / Evaluation independence

AI can assess routinely; humans calibrate periodically

A fixed rubric reduces self-graded loops. Human checks occur where field judgment matters most.

C2

Real-human discovery rep

Another person presents an ambiguous problem. Learner leads discovery without a prepared requirement list.

C5

Technical architecture review

A technically competent reviewer challenges design, assumptions, threats, alternatives and evidence.

C9

Mixed-stakeholder defence

At least one listener is not primarily technical. Learner adapts explanation and responds under challenge.

AI may roleplay customers/evaluators for repetition, but human calibration evidence is required at the three checkpoints above. AI is not treated as an independent human reviewer.

10 / Canonical stage specification

C0–C9

Each requirement now has one primary home. Historical patch explanations are removed from the main body.

C0

Canonical stage specification

Programming Independence

Code, terminal output, debugging hypotheses, reconstruction, variation and explanation.None required.
Must · pass-critical
  • Python fundamentals: values, variables, strings, numbers, booleans, collections, conditionals, loops and functions.
  • Files and JSON; exceptions; basic classes; pytest; tracebacks and debugging.
  • Shell and Git essentials.
  • Blank-file construction, reconstruction and variation without AI-generated first implementation.
Should · deepen
  • Build small personally owned programs that combine multiple C0 capabilities.
  • Maintain an error log and explain recurring mistakes from memory.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Additional Python fluency drills that stay inside C0 scope.
Stage note: No Field-SA overlay. Do not introduce agents, formal methods, black-box integration or architecture material here.
C1

Canonical stage specification

APIs, Identity, Authorization, Durable State

Integration Discovery Brief + Black-Box Integration Packet.None required.
Must · pass-critical
  • Translate a plain-language need into actors, systems, API boundaries, trust boundaries and state transitions.
  • Build and debug API interactions, identity/authentication, authorization and durable state.
  • Complete one bounded black-box integration: install/start, configure, authenticate, connect, instrument, smoke-test, break, diagnose, stop/rollback/restart, and hand over.
  • Distinguish vendor-documented behavior, experimentally observed behavior, inference and unknowns.
Should · deepen
  • Explain the same integration to an engineer and to an operational/business stakeholder.
  • Identify minimum network/data access and remove unnecessary privileges.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Integrate a second component with a different authentication or state pattern.
Stage note: Scope budget prevents the black-box exercise from displacing the core technical foundation.
C2

Canonical stage specification

Constrained Agent Runtime and Effective Authority

Agent Capability & Failure Profile + Agent Memory & State Profile + discovery record.Real-human discovery rep required at C2 before passing the stage.
Must · pass-critical
  • Build an Agent Capability & Failure Profile before assigning consequential authority.
  • Build an Agent Memory & State Profile: type, write/read/delete authority, provenance, freshness, conflict, trust, isolation, poisoning, retention, authority and recovery.
  • Distinguish the agent system, the institution, and properties that emerge only after coupling.
  • Apply effective authority: principal ∩ delegated scope ∩ task scope ∩ tool permission ∩ data boundary ∩ environment ∩ policy version.
  • Handle privacy at the control boundary: data minimization, PII/sensitive-field identification, memory-write restrictions, access boundaries and retention intent.
Should · deepen
  • Create a simple authority map and escalation map.
  • Test stale-memory or stale-authority scenarios.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Compare two agent/runtime configurations and explain how capability changes alter the safe authority envelope.
Stage note: This is the first mandatory human calibration point.
C3

Canonical stage specification

Evidence, Retrieval, Grounding, Independent Verification

Evidence & Acceptance Matrix.No new human interaction required; C2 feedback must be incorporated.
Must · pass-critical
  • Separate source of truth, model output, deterministic calculation, independent verification, audit evidence and remembered/retrieved memory.
  • Treat memory as evidence only when provenance, authority, freshness and integrity justify it.
  • Define acceptance criteria before PoC implementation.
  • Prevent stale, contradictory or unverified memory from silently overriding current institutional state.
  • Apply privacy-by-design to retrieval, memory and logs: minimum necessary data, redaction/tokenization where appropriate, access control, retention and deletion.
Should · deepen
  • Pin or version critical sources used for governed claims.
  • Define what evidence would cause the customer to reject the proposed mechanism.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Add replay or re-computation for one governed decision.
Stage note: Scheduled curriculum review occurs after C3 using execution evidence.
C4

Canonical stage specification

Reliability, Transactions, Concurrency, Cancellation, Observability

Incident Packet + Hypothesis evidence + stated properties/assumptions.None required.
Must · pass-critical
  • State at least one safety property, one eventual-progress property where applicable, and their assumptions.
  • Use Hypothesis property-based testing and stateful testing where state transitions interact.
  • Exercise retries, timeouts, crashes, overlapping requests, degraded external tools, partial persistence and stale/duplicated memory.
  • Reconcile agent memory, workflow state, tool/system state and institutional source of truth.
  • Complete the canonical lost-response/retry scenario without duplicate consequential execution.
Should · deepen
  • Conduct formal incident analysis and communicate it differently to engineering, operations/risk and an executive owner.
  • Compare example tests with generated property/stateful exploration.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Model a second reliability scenario involving cancellation or partial execution.
Stage note: Passing generated tests is evidence, not proof.
C5

Canonical stage specification

Evaluation, Security, Adversarial Testing, Threat Modelling

Eval & Regression Pack + Objection & Decision Log + Formal Assurance Packet + human review record.Technically competent architecture review required at C5.
Must · pass-critical
  • Design an eval objective, representative eval set, normal/edge/adversarial slices, baseline and regression suite.
  • Interpret false positives, false negatives and disagreement; use human-labelled references where appropriate.
  • Explain limits of LLM-as-judge: bias, leakage, correlated failure, judge drift and non-independence.
  • Threat-model prompt injection, tool misuse, stale/poisoned memory, cross-tenant leakage, credential misuse, unauthorized data access, action chaining and black-box assumptions.
  • Build one bounded TLA+ specification, run TLC on a finite model, and interpret safety/progress results and limits.
  • Demonstrate Lean proof literacy by reading, explaining and modifying a small proof; independent non-trivial Lean proof authorship is stretch, not required.
  • Perform a technically competent real-human architecture review.
Should · deepen
  • Attempt to falsify at least one important black-box integration assumption.
  • Run a regression comparison after a model/runtime change.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Author a small Lean proof independently for a precise critical claim.
Stage note: Evaluation is now a first-class discipline rather than a heading dominated by security/formal methods.
C6

Canonical stage specification

Human Authority, Maker-Checker, HITL, Stop, Reconcile, Restart

Continuous Evidence & Deviation Response Policy + HITL Capacity Sheet + authority model.No new mandatory human interaction; C5 review findings must be reflected.
Must · pass-critical
  • Build a Stakeholder & Authority Map and define who may act, approve, stop, restart, override and own incidents.
  • Design structural execution-time evidence capture independent of agent cooperation.
  • Separate continuous evidence capture, periodic audit and event-triggered review.
  • Define a Deviation Response Policy with severity, detection confidence, false-positive cost, false-negative cost, containment action, degraded mode, authority contraction, human review and restoration.
  • Stress-test containment versus continuity: a bad detector must not create an uncontrolled outage without an explicit continuity policy.
  • Quantify HITL economics: review rate, escalation rate, review duration, staffing requirement, queue latency, peak-load effect and availability implication.
  • Show the transition from universal approval toward bounded autonomy with exception-only intervention where justified.
Should · deepen
  • Run a drill where an over-sensitive deviation detector causes excessive quarantine and revise thresholds.
  • Define service-continuity tiers for deviation responses.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Prototype adaptive routing between autonomous, human-review and stop states using fixed policy.
Stage note: Scheduled curriculum review occurs after C6 using execution evidence.
C7

Canonical stage specification

Regulated BFSI Governance and Assurance

Institution → Control Traceability Matrix + verified-source register.None required.
Must · pass-critical
  • Translate policy, regulation, supervisory expectation, internal procedure, risk appetite and tacit operating practice into different architectural/control implications.
  • Build an Institution → Control Traceability Matrix.
  • Verify the current version/date of at least one horizontal AI-risk framework, one jurisdiction-specific legal/regulatory source, one sector/banking supervisory source where applicable, and one synthetic institutional policy before using them.
  • Map each source to technical interpretation, enforcement point, evidence, exception process and residual human responsibility.
  • Govern privacy and evidence retention: minimum necessary data, PII handling, log/memory retention, deletion, access and auditability.
  • Govern authority contraction/quarantine, restoration and model/runtime change approval.
Should · deepen
  • Compare two frameworks and identify where their scopes differ.
  • Create an assurance argument for one critical control.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Map the same architecture against a second jurisdiction to expose portability limits.
Stage note: Examples may include NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, model-risk-management guidance or sector-specific supervisory material, but execution must verify current authoritative versions.
C8

Canonical stage specification

SLO, Cost, Vendor and Inference Literacy

Model/Runtime Change Impact Assessment + SLO/cost decision brief.None required.
Must · pass-critical
  • Reason through latency/TTFT, throughput/concurrency, model/context size, GPU memory, HBM/DRAM/NVMe, KV cache, prefix reuse, eviction/recomputation, batching, routing, availability, capacity and cost.
  • Diagnose workload shape before recommending infrastructure.
  • Translate infrastructure choices into user experience, SLO, capex/opex and deployment risk.
  • Distinguish model-dependent behavior, runtime/orchestrator behavior and system-enforced institutional authority invariants.
  • Perform a Model/Runtime Change Impact Assessment including eval regression, tool-use behavior, memory/context behavior, latency/cost, security behavior, invalidated assumptions and controls requiring re-test/re-approval.
  • Include human-review economics in total system cost and latency, not only compute cost.
Should · deepen
  • Build a simple business case comparing credible alternatives.
  • Identify when specialist inference engineers are required.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Create a simple queue/capacity simulation for combined model + human-review bottlenecks.
Stage note: A model swap must not silently widen permissions, approval rules, data boundaries, audit requirements or stop/recovery semantics.
C9

Canonical stage specification

Integrated Hardening and Field Architecture Defence

Seven consolidated evidence bundles + transfer-test score + mixed-stakeholder defence.Mixed-stakeholder real-human presentation/defence required at C9.
Must · pass-critical
  • Run one full ambiguous Vendor-Side Role B engagement from discovery through adoption/operations.
  • Use the canonical sequence: Discover → Frame → Require → Characterize → Integrate → Architect → Prove → Build → Specify/Check/Interpret → Communicate → Defend → Adopt → Operate → Learn.
  • Revise earlier artifacts rather than recreate them from scratch.
  • Handle one change injection and one incident injection.
  • Complete a mixed-stakeholder real-human defence.
  • Complete a bounded graded transfer test in an unfamiliar non-banking or different regulated domain to demonstrate architecture transfer rather than banking memorization.
Should · deepen
  • Include a model/runtime or vendor-component change in the engagement.
  • Re-run the eval/regression suite after a meaningful change.
Stretch · non-blocking
  • Add a second transfer scenario or a deeper implementation in the transfer domain.
Stage note: Scheduled curriculum review occurs after C9. C9 is integration and revision, not a 20+ document writing exercise.

Discovery doctrine

Substantial scenarios remain intentionally incomplete. The evaluator/customer does not provide all relevant requirements up front. The learner must identify ambiguity, distinguish symptom from root problem, ask before designing, summarize and confirm understanding, and record assumptions and unresolved unknowns.

Learning covenant

Generated implementation is not learned capability. The learner writes, debugs, runs, interprets and owns the work.

Before debugging help: “My hypothesis is: ___”. After substantial help: blank file → reproduce → variation.

Capability advances through demonstrated evidence, not confidence, familiarity, reading time or explanation alone.

AI may explain, question, review, challenge, suggest bounded hints after an attempt, generate adversarial tests and roleplay stakeholders. AI may not provide the learner’s first implementation, silently rewrite active work, reveal the full hidden requirement set before discovery, or supply the final defence before a learner attempt.

14 / Curriculum governance

Stable against noise; correctable when evidence demands it

The prior freeze wording is replaced by two explicit review paths.

Path A · Material gap

Correct at any time

Command may authorize the smallest bounded correction when a significant capability gap, material mismatch or execution blocker is demonstrated.

Path B · Scheduled execution review

After C3, C6 and C9

Review execution evidence for scope mismatch, excess load, missing capability, weak rubric or invalid assumption. Review does not imply automatic change.

Do not reopen for noise: individual job postings, vendor announcements, framework fashion, LinkedIn posts or market anxiety are not curriculum-change triggers by themselves.
Appendix A

Change history — condensed

The main curriculum is canonical. Historical changes live here only for traceability.

v0.3 → v0.6 history

v0.3: added Field Solution Architecture: discovery, framing, stakeholder management, PoC, communication, adoption and operating model.

v0.7 — canonicalization & execution hardening

v0.7 does not add a new stage or new target role. It consolidates the specification and makes execution more measurable and feasible.

  • replaces patch-stacking with one canonical specification
  • adds Must / Should / Stretch scope budgets rather than stage deadlines
  • adds four-level measurable gate rubrics and critical-fail conditions
  • adds fixed-rubric AI assessment plus human calibration at C2, C5 and C9
  • adds explicit eval-set design, regression testing and LLM-as-judge limitations
  • adds regulatory-source verification practice without freezing mutable frameworks into the curriculum
  • adds HITL staffing/throughput economics
  • adds explicit privacy/data-minimization requirements for memory, retrieval and logs
  • adds containment-vs-continuity stress testing for deviation policies
  • adds a bounded cross-domain transfer test at C9
  • consolidates C9 into seven evidence bundles instead of 20+ fresh artifacts
  • replaces the old freeze wording with material-gap and scheduled-review paths
  • reduces Lean to mandatory proof literacy; non-trivial proof authorship becomes stretch
  • preserves C0 unchanged and adds no C10
